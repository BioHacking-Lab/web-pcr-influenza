01 The problem · One Health

Avian Influenza A is no longer only a poultry problem.

Record outbreaks are culling flocks and spilling into mammals. Containing them hinges on one thing: detecting the right virus early and telling it apart from close relatives. Today's multiplexed PCR panels hit a specificity ceiling — not because of the chemistry, but because of how their targets are chosen.

05 The proposal · multiplex PCR for influenza A

Complementary targets, not more targets.

One common signal says influenza A is there. A combination of discriminant signals says which subtype — when a validated interpretation rule allows it.

Why combine signals? Reassortment.

Influenza segments reassort: an offspring can carry one segment from the target lineage and seven from another. A single signal reads one segment, so it cannot tell them apart; signals on distinct segments add complementary evidence.
